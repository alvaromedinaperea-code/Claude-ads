# Product Marketing Context — CIRCLE

*Last updated: 2026-05-06*

---

## Product Overview

**One-liner:**
Circle is the app where you post a sport activity and find real people to do it with you.

**What it does:**
Circle lets users propose sport activities (running, cycling, tennis, etc.), publish them to their local community or their circle, and have other people join them. The focus is on the coordination *before* the activity happens — not tracking it after. Clubs can also use Circle to manage members, post group activities, and track engagement.

**Product category:**
Social sports coordination / Group fitness app

**Product type:**
Mobile app (B2C + B2B lite) — freemium SaaS

**Business model:**
Three revenue streams:
1. **Free tier** — Post and join activities with limited features (radius cap on discovery, limited KPI visibility)
2. **Premium subscription** — Full feature access for individual users + club management tier for organizations
3. **Boosted Activities** — Pay-per-boost to promote a posted activity to a wider audience (like a promoted post)

**Premium features include:**
- Unlimited discovery radius (see activities everywhere, not capped by distance)
- Full social KPIs: km run together, who you trained with, weekly streak, activity history
- Club tier: member management, member intent data (why people joined), club-level analytics and dashboards

## Target Audience

**Primary users:**
Active individuals aged 18–40 who play sports regularly and want to find people to train with — not just track solo workouts.

**Secondary users:**
Club managers and sports organizers who need a tool to post group activities, attract new members, and understand their community.

**Primary use case:**
"I want to go for a run on Sunday morning and I don't want to go alone — who else is doing this?"

**Jobs to be done:**
- *Find training partners*: "Help me find someone to run/cycle/play tennis with near me, now or this weekend"
- *Organize group activities*: "Help me post an activity so others can join me easily"
- *Build a sports community*: "Help me grow and manage my running club or sports group"
- *Track social fitness*: "Show me how active I've been and who I've trained with"

**Use cases:**
- Solo runner posts a Saturday 10K and 5 people join
- Running club posts their weekly session and manages RSVPs
- New person in a city discovers local sports communities
- Club organizer sees which members are most active and why people joined

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:**
Finding people to do sport with is surprisingly hard. Most people either train alone or rely on fragmented WhatsApp groups and word of mouth.

**Why current alternatives fall short:**
- **Strava** is a *post-activity* tracker — it doesn't help you find people to do the activity *with* before it happens
- **Meetup** is too generic, not built for recurring sports activities or real-time coordination
- **WhatsApp groups** are chaotic — no discovery, no structure, no data
- **Tribe** (closest competitor) — still maturing, less focus on club management and social KPIs

**What it costs them:**
- Training less because they can't find partners → lower consistency, lower motivation
- Club organizers waste hours on manual coordination every week
- New members to clubs have no context on who they're joining or why others joined

**Emotional tension:**
"I want to be active and social but I don't know who else is doing this near me." / Fear of showing up to a group and not fitting in.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct competitor:**
- **Tribe** — Social fitness / group activity coordination. Falls short: less polished club management, weaker KPI layer, smaller community.

**Secondary competitors (same problem, different solution):**
- **WhatsApp + Google Forms** — The default for most clubs. Falls short: no discovery, no member analytics, messy to manage.
- **Meetup** — General community events. Falls short: not sport-specific, no training partner matching, high friction.

**Indirect competitors (different framing):**
- **Strava** — Post-activity tracking and social proof. Falls short: doesn't help you *plan* or *find people* before the activity.
- **Garmin/Apple Fitness+** — Solo training ecosystem. Falls short: no social coordination layer at all.

**Key insight:** Circle owns the moment *before* the activity. Strava owns after. This is a distinct and underserved space.

---

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**
- Only app built around *proposing* an activity first, then letting others join
- Both sides of the market: individual users + club management in one product
- Social KPIs that track *who you trained with* (not just what you did) — unique layer
- Boost mechanic: activities can be promoted like ads, creating a network effect incentive
- Unlimited discovery radius on premium — find activities anywhere, not just 5km away

**How we do it differently:**
Activity is the unit of value, not the workout log. You post what you're going to do → people join → you do it together → you both track it.

**Why customers choose Circle over alternatives:**
"I actually found people to run with" — the core promise fulfilled in one session.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "My area won't have users" | Boosted activities and the unlimited radius on premium mean even early adopters can find people; clubs help seed local density |
| "I already use Strava" | Circle is what happens *before* you open Strava — we're complementary, not competing |
| "Why not just use a WhatsApp group?" | No discovery (strangers can't find you), no KPIs, no member management — WhatsApp is a chat tool, not a sports coordination platform |
| "Is my data private?" | You control visibility — post publicly or only to your circle |

**Anti-persona:**
Elite competitive athletes who train solo and don't want social interaction around training. People who only want a GPS/performance tracker.

## Switching Dynamics (JTBD Four Forces)

**Push** (frustrations pushing them away from current tools):
- Training alone because WhatsApp plans always fall through
- Club organizers spending hours managing sessions manually
- No way to meet new people in a new city without formal club membership

**Pull** (what draws them to Circle):
- "Post and people come to you" — zero effort coordination
- See km trained together, streaks, who you ran with — social proof and motivation
- Clubs get real member data for the first time

**Habit** (what keeps them stuck with current tools):
- Everyone is already in the WhatsApp group
- Strava is the default for any social fitness sharing

**Anxiety** (worries about switching):
- "What if I post an activity and nobody joins?" (solution: Boost, and initial seeding by clubs)
- "Another app to install" — integration with Apple Health / Strava reduces friction

**Current stage:** Pre-scale / early traction — focus on activation and retention before paid acquisition.
